## The structure we're stealing

**1. Covers are series, not one-offs.** Oren runs franchises: *Product vs Brand: X*, *The Luxury Codes: X*, *Creative Direction 101 (subtitle)*. A series lets people recognise the post before they read it. Bassonism gets four:

| Series | What it is | Oren equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| **Behind the Brief** | A real project, told as a story | Meet the Bots, The Keys to Creative Directing an Event |
| **Cyber, But Make It Culture** | A contrarian verdict on cyber marketing | Why Is B2B Marketing So Boring, Your Customer Avatar Is Wrong |
| **AI Video 101** | Craft and process, useful enough to save | Art Directing Your Content, 7 Viral One Shot Formats |
| **From the Vault** | Old work and origin stories | The Vault, Kid Adults |

**2. Cover anatomy.** A huge headline (condensed sans or editorial serif), Ohad's face or the hero object cut in, and a small **kicker** line at the bottom with an arrow. The kicker is the hook's second half: *"Here's how you actually do it ->"*, *"And your ads are paying for it"*, *"these creators prove it ->"*.

**3. The arc inside** (8-10 slides):
1. **Cover** - the claim or the title
2. **Tension** - the pain, the problem, the thing everyone does wrong
3. **Reframe** - the one sentence that flips it
4-7. **The meat** - steps, scenes or proof, one idea per slide
8. **The receipt** - the result, the number, the moment
9. **Payoff line** - the thing to screenshot
10. **CTA** - save / send / DM a word

**4. Rules.** One idea per slide. Max ~25 words per slide. Name the tools and the people. The kicker always promises something the slides deliver.

## 10. The USP Map
**Series:** Cyber, But Make It Culture · **Source:** Astelia USP Workshop framework

**Cover:** THE USP MAP
**Kicker:** 4 zones. Only one makes money ->
**Visual:** A three-circle Venn (You / Them / The buyer) with four zones labelled.

1. The USP Map. 4 zones. Only one makes money ->
2. Draw three circles: what your brand does well, what your competitor does well, what the buyer actually wants.
3. Winning Zone: you're great at it, the buyer wants it, rivals aren't there. Make it bigger.
4. Risky Zone: you and a rival both deliver what the buyer wants. You can win, but only with emotion, innovation or sharper execution.
5. Losing Zone: a rival meets the need better than you. Don't plant your flag here.
6. Who Cares Zone: a fight over something the buyer doesn't value. A fun way to waste your budget.
7. The test for whatever survives: Unique (only you can say it), Verifiable (you can prove it), Attributable (it points to you), Appreciated (the buyer wants it).
8. Then cut. 8 truths on the wall. 3 survive. 2 after testing against rivals. 1.
9. Most brands are camped in Who Cares and wondering why nobody's buying.
10. Save it. Run it with your team this week.

**Caption:** The exact framework I run in USP workshops with cyber startups. Steal it. #Positioning #CybersecurityMarketing
**Flags:** None, as long as Astelia specifics stay out.
